Floatees / Uncharted Seas: data sources, licences and citations
==================================================================

On the real Earth the game's ocean is driven by observations. Everything in floatees/data/ is produced offline by
the scripts in floatees/tools/ (Python 3 + numpy, numcodecs, netCDF4, pyshp, Pillow); the page itself never calls a
data server. Regenerate with (CACHE is any scratch directory):

  tools/fetch_gdp.py CACHE/gdp                 (~1.6 GB from AWS, no login)
  tools/fetch_winds.py CACHE/wind              (~1.6 GB from NOAA CoastWatch ERDDAP, no login)
  tools/build_land.py NE_DIR CACHE             (Natural Earth shapefiles unzipped in NE_DIR)
  tools/build_currents.py CACHE/gdp CACHE      (needs data/land-q.bin)
  tools/build_winds.py CACHE
  tools/build_slip.py CACHE/gdp CACHE          (prints the surface-slip coefficients hard-coded as SLIP in index.html)
  tools/build_textures.py IMG_DIR CACHE        (IMG_DIR: Blue Marble JPEG and ETOPO1 netCDF, see below)
  tools/build_tracks.py CACHE/gdp              (the real drifter tracks overlay)

Files (3.6 MB in total; the drift itself needs 1.9 MB; phones skip earth-4k.jpg, 2.3 MB; tracks.bin loads only when shown):
  data/currents.bin  1.2 MB  15 m surface currents, 0.5 deg, mean + annual + semi-annual harmonics; eddy diffusivity, 2 deg
  data/winds.bin     0.7 MB  10 m wind, 1 deg, 12 monthly means + synoptic standard deviation
  data/land-q.bin    11 kB   land/sea mask, 0.25 deg
  data/earth-2k.jpg  0.3 MB  globe colour, 2048x1024 (loaded first)
  data/earth-4k.jpg  1.1 MB  globe colour, 4096x2048 (desktop only)
  data/relief-2k.jpg 0.1 MB  land relief for the renderer's bump map
  data/tracks.bin    0.2 MB  240 real GDP drifter tracks (one year each, 2-day fixes) for the "real drifter tracks" overlay


1. Surface currents and eddy diffusivity: NOAA Global Drifter Program (public domain, US Government work)
   Lumpkin, R.; Centurioni, L. (2019). NOAA Global Drifter Program quality-controlled 6-hour interpolated data from
   ocean surface drifting buoys. NOAA National Centers for Environmental Information. https://doi.org/10.25921/7ntx-z961
   Version December 2024 (1979-02-15 to 2025-01-20; 28,706 drifters; 48.8 million 6-hourly fixes), read from the NOAA
   Open Data Dissemination bucket s3://noaa-oar-hourly-gdp-pds/experimental/gdp6h_ragged_dec24.zarr ("freely available").
   Processing (tools/build_currents.py): only fixes with the 15 m drogue attached (21.6 million); per 0.5 deg bin a
   least-squares fit of mean + annual + semi-annual harmonics (after Lumpkin & Johnson 2013, J. Geophys. Res. 118, 2992;
   Laurindo, Mariano & Lumpkin 2017, Deep-Sea Res. I 124, 73), with adaptive Gaussian smoothing of the normal
   equations (>= 400 fixes) and a ridge on the harmonics; gaps (sea ice) filled by Laplace interpolation.
   Eddy diffusivity: Davis (1991, J. Mar. Res. 49, 711) single-particle diffusivity, the integral of the lagged covariance
   of each drifter's residual velocity, averaged over lags 3-5 days, in 2 deg bins (cf. Zhurbas & Oh 2004, J. Geophys.
   Res. 109, C05015). Result: median 3,200 m2/s, 5-95 % range 820-14,700 m2/s; Lagrangian time scale 1.9 days.
   Surface slip (tools/build_slip.py): undrogued minus drogued drifters regressed on CCMP wind: 1.28 % of wind speed
   (3 deg right of the wind) north of 5N, 1.32 % (4 deg left) south of 5S; cf. Pazan & Niiler (2001, J. Atmos. Oceanic
   Technol. 18, 476), about 1 %, and Lumpkin et al. (2013, J. Atmos. Oceanic Technol. 30, 2797), about 50 % higher.
   The AOML-hosted climatology of Laurindo et al. (2017) itself could not be downloaded from this build environment:
   www.aoml.noaa.gov returned HTTP 403 (CloudFront) to scripted requests, and erddap.aoml.noaa.gov closed the
   connection. The climatology here is therefore recomputed from the same raw GDP data.

2. Winds: RSS CCMP V3.1 (Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International)
   Mears, C.; Lee, T.; Ricciardulli, L.; Wang, X.; Wentz, F. (2022). RSS Cross-Calibrated Multi-Platform (CCMP)
   6-hourly ocean vector wind analysis on 0.25 deg grid, Version 3.1. Remote Sensing Systems, Santa Rosa, CA.
   https://doi.org/10.56236/RSS-uv6h31 . CCMP Version-3.1 vector wind analyses are produced by Remote Sensing Systems.
   Data are available at www.remss.com. Read through NOAA CoastWatch ERDDAP (dataset ccmp_31), every 14th 6-hourly field
   1993-2023 on a 1 deg grid (~3,200 fields), averaged per calendar month.

3. Leeway (the wind's push on each floating object): US Coast Guard search-and-rescue leeway taxonomy (US Government
   work). Allen, A.A.; Plourde, J.V. (1999). Review of Leeway: Field Experiments and Implementation. US Coast Guard R&D
   Center, Report CG-D-08-99. Allen, A.A. (2005). Leeway Divergence. Report CG-D-05-05, Tables 3-1 and 3-2 (taken from
   the copy distributed with OpenDrift, opendrift/models/OBJECTPROP.DAT). Classes used (zero-intercept slopes):
   bath toys: "medical waste, vials, small", downwind 2.95 %, crosswind +-0.54 % of the 10 m wind; after the toys
   leaked (Ebbesmeyer et al. 2007) relaxing with an e-folding time of 270 days towards
   sneakers / waterlogged toys: "person in water, vertical", downwind 0.48 %, crosswind +-0.15 %.
   Synoptic gusts add a diffusivity (leeway x sigma_wind)^2 x 2 days. Beaching: e-folding 4 days in sea cells touching
   the coast (cf. Onink et al. 2021, Environ. Res. Lett. 16, 064053); immediate on reaching land.
   Wave-driven Stokes drift is not added separately: Coast Guard leeway coefficients are measured relative to the
   near-surface water and already include the drift caused by wind and waves on the object (Allen & Plourde 1999).
   A Stokes drift climatology (ERA5 "u/v-component of surface Stokes drift") needs a Copernicus Climate Data Store
   account and API key (cds.climate.copernicus.eu), which this build environment did not have.

4. Coastlines: Natural Earth 1:10m physical vectors, version 5 (public domain). https://www.naturalearthdata.com/
   ne_10m_land, ne_10m_minor_islands, ne_10m_antarctic_ice_shelves_polys, ne_10m_lakes, rasterised at 0.25 deg
   (land when at least half of 64 samples in the cell are land).
   The idealised model (Earth with its spin changed) still uses the old 1 deg mask from Natural Earth 1:110m land
   polygons via the world-atlas package (ISC, Copyright 2012-2019 Michael Bostock).

5. Globe imagery: NASA Blue Marble Next Generation with topography and bathymetry, July 2004 (NASA Earth
   Observatory, public domain). Stockli, R.; Vermote, E.; Saleous, N.; Simmon, R.; Herring, D. (2005). The Blue Marble
   Next Generation: a true color earth dataset including seasonal dynamics from MODIS. NASA Earth Observatory.
   Relief: ETOPO1 1 arc-minute global relief model (ice surface), read at 5' from NOAA CoastWatch ERDDAP (etopo180).
   Amante, C.; Eakins, B.W. (2009). ETOPO1 1 Arc-Minute Global Relief Model. NOAA Technical Memorandum NESDIS NGDC-24.
   https://doi.org/10.7289/V5C8276M (public domain).

6. The events the drift is checked against (tests/floatees.test.js)
   Ebbesmeyer, C.C.; Ingraham, W.J. (1992). Shoe spill in the North Pacific. Eos 73(34), 361. (Hansa Carrier, 27 May
   1990, 48N 161W; shoes ashore from southern Oregon to the Queen Charlotte Islands six months to a year later.)
   Ebbesmeyer, C.C.; Ingraham, W.J. (1994). Pacific toy spill fuels ocean current pathways research. Eos 75(37), 425.
   (10 January 1992, 44.7N 178.1E; toys near Sitka ten months later.)
   Ebbesmeyer, C.C.; Ingraham, W.J.; Royer, T.C.; Grosch, C.E. (2007). Tub toys orbit the Pacific Subarctic Gyre. Eos
   88(1), 1. (Toys beached near Sitka in August-September 1992; windage "substantial" for a quarter of the first orbit.)
   van Sebille, E.; England, M.H.; Froyland, G. (2012). Origin, dynamics and evolution of ocean garbage patches from
   observed surface drifters. Environ. Res. Lett. 7, 044040.
   Maximenko, N.; Hafner, J.; Niiler, P. (2012). Pathways of marine debris derived from trajectories of Lagrangian
   drifters. Mar. Pollut. Bull. 65, 51-62.
   Lebreton, L. et al. (2018). Evidence that the Great Pacific Garbage Patch is rapidly accumulating plastic. Sci. Rep.
   8, 4666.

Generated worlds (Uncharted Seas) and Earth with its spin stopped or reversed use the idealised Stommel (1948) model,
since no observations of them can exist.
