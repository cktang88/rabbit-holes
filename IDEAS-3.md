# Round 3: 56 candidates from a wide research sweep

Sources:
- About 50 `Special:Random` Wikipedia pages, plus Wikipedia's Unusual Articles and list-of-paradoxes pages.
- A survey of neal.fun, Bartosz Ciechanowski, Nicky Case, Red Blob Games, Show HN toys from 2025–26 and incremental games.
- A sweep of counterintuitive phenomena.
- A game-feel and graphics playbook (see `PLAYBOOK.md`).

Round 3 has a new rule: **the play surface is a canvas or WebGL, never DOM panels.**

Gaps the research found, compared with the 16 experiments already built:
- Earth processes: water, rock, weather.
- Infrastructure systems: traffic, the power grid.
- Probability that defies intuition.
- Fluids.
- Perception.
- Crafts.
- Explorables that have real goals (Opus Magnum-style histograms are largely unclaimed).

Scores are 1–5 for **H**ook, **D**epth, **V**isual potential and **N**ovelty against our set and the web. ★ marks the shortlist.

## Build order (round 3)
1. **Watershed** (three.js terrain, erosion, water shaders). The graphics showcase, and it fills the Earth-processes gap.
2. **Gridlock**: Mini Metro look, Braess puzzles. Fills the infrastructure gap.
3. **Ruin**: thousands of glowing wealth paths. The most mind-bending probability idea.
4. **Snowfall**: a hex-lattice crystal grown in real time with an iridescent render. Very shareable.
5. **Low Reynolds**: shader-driven micro-world swimming.

Then: Floatees, MONIAC, Polarizer, Chladni, Trail.

## Status
Built: Watershed, Gridlock, Ruin, Snowfall, Low Reynolds, Floatees, Chladni. Every one was verified by script: goals reachable, not solved at the start. The hub was also redesigned (particle-tunnel canvas, glass cards).
Next up: Polarizer (the quantum bomb tester), MONIAC (the water computer), Trail (bike stability), Square–Cube.
